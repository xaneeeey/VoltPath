# Battery and charging diagnostic trouble codes

## P0A7E - Hybrid/EV battery pack over temperature
The battery management system measured a pack temperature above its safe limit (typically 55-60 °C). Immediate action: stop DC fast charging, reduce load, park in shade and let the pack cool. If the code repeats within a week, suspect a failing cooling pump, blocked coolant loop or a defective cell module; book a service inspection. Repeated over-temperature events accelerate state-of-health loss.

## P0A7F - Hybrid/EV battery pack deterioration
The pack's usable capacity or internal resistance has degraded beyond the manufacturer threshold. Compare the vehicle's SoH trend with its model cohort; if it is losing capacity several times faster than peers, raise a warranty claim. Limit DC fast charging and avoid storing the vehicle at 100 % SoC.

## P0A80 - Replace hybrid/EV battery pack
One or more cell blocks are out of tolerance. The vehicle may enter limp mode. Schedule a pack replacement or module repair; keep the vehicle off long routes until then.

## P0AA6 - High-voltage isolation fault
Insulation resistance between the high-voltage system and chassis is too low. Safety critical: take the vehicle out of service and have it inspected by a certified HV technician before driving or charging.

## P0B24 - Cell voltage imbalance
Cells within the pack have diverged in voltage. A slow AC charge to 100 % allows the BMS to balance cells; if imbalance persists after two balancing charges, inspect the pack.

## P0D27 - Charger input voltage too low
The on-board charger saw low supply voltage. Usually a site or charger issue rather than a vehicle fault; try a different connector and report the station to the operator.
